A tool dragged across a ridged surface: each ridge gives a tick, and the ticks blur into a buzz as the speed rises. It's the same thing that happens when you run a stick along railings: slow, you hear every bar; fast, you hear a note.

**Try it:** load **Drones → Chain Drag Drone**, hold a note and sweep **Drag Speed**: slow drags tick, fast ones buzz.

## What it is

An exciter drags across corrugated metal. The ridges push it up and down at a regular rate, so the force has a rhythm set by the speed and the ridge spacing.

## How it sounds

Rhythmic and mechanical: ratchets, conveyors, washboards. Slow, it's a series of clicks; fast, a pitched rattle.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Corrugated Drag" />

- **Drag Speed:** how fast the exciter crosses the ridges. Higher raises the ridge rate.
- **Ridge Spacing:** the distance between ridges. Higher spaces the ticks further apart.
- **Ridge Depth:** the depth of the ridges. Higher gives stronger, louder ticks; at 0 there are none.
- **Exciter Mass:** the weight of the dragged exciter.

**Speed** scales **Drag Speed**; **Roughness** doesn't affect it.

## Pairings

On **Chain** it gives a dragged-chain drone; on **Plate** or **Sheet Metal**, a ratchety buzz; on **Pipe**, a hollow clatter.

## Tips

- **Drag Speed** and **Ridge Spacing** together set the tick rate: halve one or double the other for the same rhythm.
- **Release snap:** the drag holds the object out of its rest position, and letting go quickly makes it spring back. With Corrugated Drag on a Plate and the default MSEG **Release** of <Data param="mseg_release" field="default" /> s, the half second after you let go is about 15 dB louder than the held note; at 0.5 s the snap is gone.
- Somewhere between separate ticks and a buzz there's a rattling sweet spot. Sweep **Drag Speed** slowly to find it for your object.

## Presets to try

- **Drones → Chain Drag Drone**
- **Experimental → Corrugated Drag**: one preset on each of the nine objects.
