A light stick that hits and bounces back: a sharp tick, followed by a few quick re-strikes that get closer together.

**Try it:** load **Percussion → Plate Snare Crack**, then raise **Micro Bounce Limit** and play again: the stick comes back more times after the first hit.

## What it is

A light, stiff stick hits the object with a hard contact. In the first contact it chatters against the surface for a few samples. Then, like a stick dropped on a table, it comes back: it re-strikes the object **Micro Bounce Limit** minus one times, each re-strike weaker than the last and each flight shorter, by the factor **Restitution Bounciness**. The first flight lasts about 40 ms times **Restitution Bounciness**.

## How it sounds

Snappy and bright, with a quick ruffle after the hit. On a plate it gives a snare crack; on a cog, a tick; on a beam, a knock. The re-strikes are what make it sound like a stick rather than a hammer.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Drumstick" />

- **Stick Mass:** heavier sticks drive the object harder and brighter.
- **Tip Stiffness:** higher gives a sharper, slightly brighter tick.
- **Restitution Bounciness:** how much the stick bounces back. Higher makes the re-strikes stronger and spreads them out further.
- **Micro Bounce Limit:** how many times the stick comes back. Higher gives a buzzier, rattling attack; the extra re-strikes also damp the object a little.

## Pairings

Small, bright objects suit it: **Plate** for snares, **Industrial Cog** for ticks, **Sheet Metal** for crashes. Nine of the ten factory drum kits use Drumstick pads for snares, hats and cymbals.

## Tips

- For a single clean tick, set **Micro Bounce Limit** to its minimum.
- For a ruff or a dropped-stick roll, raise both **Micro Bounce Limit** and **Restitution Bounciness**.
