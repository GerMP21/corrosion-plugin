A rigid mallet thrown at the object: a hard, bright contact that brings out the high modes. The shorter a contact, the higher the frequencies it can excite, and a hard mallet is over almost before it starts. Reach for it when a sound needs to cut.

**Try it:** load **Lead → Hard Cable Pluck**, then turn **Material Stiffness** up and down between notes.

## What it is

A solid mallet head hits the surface, pushes into it with a stiff, slightly lossy contact, and rebounds. The contact is solved together with the object's response, so a hard hit stays stable however stiff the mallet is.

## How it sounds

Brighter and harder than Felt Mallet, with a clear attack: clangs, plucks and metallic hits. It excites more of the object's high modes, so inharmonic objects like **Sheet Metal** and **Chain** sound more like themselves.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Hard Mallet" />

- **Mallet Mass:** heavier mallets stay in contact longer and bring out the low modes.
- **Material Stiffness:** higher gives a sharper, slightly brighter strike.
- **Impact Damping:** the energy lost in the contact. Higher softens and shortens the attack; on a plate the ring is about half as long at the maximum.

## Pairings

On **Taut Cable** it plucks; on **Plate** it gives a struck lead; on **Sheet Metal** and **Chain**, clangs. The factory bank also uses it for a driven **I-Beam** bass and a sheet-metal hi-hat.

## Tips

- **Impact Damping** is the control to reach for when a hit is too long or too clicky.
- For hi-hats and ticks, use a small, bright object and a short **Release**.
- Hard contacts excite everything, including the partials you might not want. If a hit is too harsh, lower the object's **Brightness** before you change the mallet.
