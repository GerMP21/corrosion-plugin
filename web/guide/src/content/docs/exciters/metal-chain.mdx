A chain dropped on the object: a cascade of link strikes and the clatter of links rattling against each other.

**Try it:** load **Percussion → Chain Rattle Hit**, then raise **Link Count** and **Drop Envelope Spread** and play again.

## What it is

A chain falls onto the object. Its links land one after another, spread over a short time, and each one strikes the metal; between the strikes, the links rattle against each other. Heavier links hit harder and dent the surface further.

## How it sounds

Busy, metallic and rhythmic: rattles, clatter and junk-metal hits. It's the most chaotic of the strikes, and each note is a little different.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Metal Chain" />

- **Link Count:** the number of links in the chain. Higher gives a longer, busier rattle.
- **Chain Mass:** the weight of the links. Heavier links strike harder.
- **Drop Envelope Spread:** how spread out in time the links land. Higher stretches the cascade.
- **Internal Rattle:** the rattling between the links. Higher adds more clatter. At 0 you hear only the links' strikes.
- **Rattle Color:** where the rattle's high-pass sits. Higher thins the rattle out and leaves the links' strikes.

**Speed** shortens the drop, so the links land sooner, and **Roughness** scales **Internal Rattle**.

## Pairings

On **Chain** it sounds like a chain on a chain; on **Plate** or **Sheet Metal**, like scrap falling on a sheet. Four factory kits use it for clatter and junk percussion.

## Tips

- For a single clank, lower **Link Count** and **Drop Envelope Spread**.
- For a long rattle, raise both and add a long **Release**.

## Presets to try

- **Percussion → Chain Rattle Hit**
- **Experimental → Metal Chain**: one preset on each of the nine objects.
