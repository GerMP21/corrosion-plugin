A pipe used as a hammer: it strikes the object and rings itself while it does, so you hear two pieces of metal at once. It's the clang of a wrench dropped on a girder, the anvil in the forge, and it's the only exciter that brings a pitch of its own.

**Try it:** load **Percussion → Beam Anvil** and play the same note while you move **Pipe Pitch**.

## What it is

A stiff metal pipe hits the object. Unlike the other strikes, the striker has its own small set of modes, so it rings too: the contact force drives both the object and the pipe. The result is two coupled metals, like an anvil hit with a hammer.

## How it sounds

Hard, clanging and metallic, with an extra ring on top of the object's. It suits anvils, boings, clanks and metallic plucks.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Metal Pipe" />

- **Pipe Mass:** heavier pipes hit louder and brighter.
- **Metal Stiffness:** higher gives a harder, brighter clang.
- **Pipe Pitch:** the tuning of the striking pipe's own ring, which colors the strike.
- **Pipe Ring Decay:** how long the striking pipe rings.

## Pairings

On **I-Beam** it gives an anvil; on **Taut Cable**, a metallic pluck. Two factory kits use it for clanks and cowbell-like hits.

## Tips

- Tune **Pipe Pitch** by ear against the note you play: some settings ring with the object, others against it.
- Raise **Metal Stiffness** for a brighter clang.
- Two pitches at once can clash beautifully or just clash. For tuned parts, find a **Pipe Pitch** that agrees with the notes you play most and keep it there.

## Presets to try

- **Percussion → Beam Anvil**
- **Lead → Metal Pipe Pluck**
- **Experimental → Metal Pipe**: one preset on each of the nine objects.
