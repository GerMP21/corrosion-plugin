A brush of wires landing on the object over a few milliseconds: a swish instead of a single hit.

**Try it:** load **Percussion → Brush Plate Swish** and turn **Spread Duration** up: the swish stretches into a sweep.

## What it is

Instead of one contact, Wire Brush fires a cloud of tiny impacts, one per wire, spread over a short time, then brightens them like the bristly edge of a real brush. It's a noise-like exciter: no two hits are exactly alike.

## How it sounds

Soft, airy and textured: brushed snares, hats, shakers and swishes. On a plate it sounds like brushes on a snare; on a cable, a scratchy pluck.

## Controls

<ModelControls exciter="Wire Brush" />

- **Wire Density:** the number of wires. Higher gives a denser, smoother swish.
- **Spread Duration:** how long the wires take to land. Higher smears the hit into a sweep, and makes it louder.
- **Wire Stiffness:** higher sounds brighter, tighter and louder.
- **Amplitude Randomization:** random level differences between wires. Higher sounds looser and more organic.

**Speed** shortens the spread, so the brush lands sooner, and **Roughness** scales **Amplitude Randomization**.

## Pairings

**Plate** and **Sheet Metal** give brushed snares and hats; **Taut Cable** and **Coil Spring**, scratchy textures. Three factory kits use it for hats and brushed snares.

## Tips

- For a crisp hat, keep **Spread Duration** short and **Wire Stiffness** high.
- A long **Spread Duration** with a long **Release** makes a sweep that works as a sound effect.

## Presets to try

- **Percussion → Brush Plate Swish**
- **Experimental → Wire Brush**: one preset on each of the nine objects.
