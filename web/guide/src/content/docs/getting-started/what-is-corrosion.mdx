Corrosion sounds like metal because it simulates metal: each note sets a virtual object vibrating, and you hear the object ring.

**Try it:** open the [web app](https://corrosion-vst.org/play/), click **Start audio** and press the `A` key on your computer keyboard. You hear a hand strike a pipe.

## Physical modelling in a minute

Most synthesizers start from an oscillator and filter it. Corrosion starts from two physical things and lets them interact:

- **The exciter** is what plays the object: a hand, a mallet, a bow, a chain, a jet of air. There are <Data count="exciters" /> of them, in three families. *Struck* exciters hit once and let go. *Friction* exciters (a bow, a scraper, a grinder) keep rubbing while the key is down. *Specialty* exciters blow air, hum, snap or rain particles onto the object.
- **The object** is what gets played: a pipe, a plate, a tank, a chain, a beam, a cable, a spring, a sheet or a cog. There are <Data count="objects" /> of them. Each one is a set of resonances, the *modes*, that ring at frequencies set by its shape and size.

The two are coupled both ways. The object pushes back on the exciter, so a bow really sticks and slips on the surface it plays, and a mallet bounces off. That is where the life in the sound comes from: you don't draw the sound, you set up a situation and the physics plays it out.

After the voice, an output chain shapes the result: a filter, drive, a resonant body, stereo spread, a room, spring or echo, a diode clipper and a limiter. [How it sounds](/wiki/how-it-sounds/) follows a note through the whole path.

A few consequences are worth knowing from the start:

- **Size sets the pitch.** The key you play and the object's size together set the note: raising the size control lowers the pitch, on every object. Each object names that control after its own shape (Pipe Length, Plate Size, Beam Mass…).
- **Struck metal rings.** A struck pipe, beam or cable can ring for seconds. That's the object, not a reverb.
- **Friction notes behave like gestures.** A bowed note follows its force envelope, and letting go snaps the object free, which can be louder than the held note.

## What you can make

The factory bank has <Data count="factoryPresets" /> presets, organised the way you'd look for them:

- **Bass, Lead, Chords, Drones and Percussion**, designed by hand and checked to do what their folder says: basses keep their weight low, chords can be played as chords, drones hold while the key is down, percussion dies away on its own.
- **Drums**: <Data count="drumKits" /> drum kits on the General MIDI layout, where each key plays its own sound.
- **Experimental**: one preset for every exciter on every object, drawn at random within playable ranges. They're a map of what the engine can do, and a good place to find raw material.

Corrosion is tuned for damaged metal, friction, weight and mechanical tension. It is not a classic synthesizer, and it has no audio input: it's an instrument you play with notes.
