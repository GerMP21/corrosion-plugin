title: "Glossary"

The words this guide uses, in plain language. If a page uses a term you don't know, it's probably here.

**Try it:** skim the list once. Half of these you'll recognise from other synths; the other half are where physical modelling gets interesting.

## Terms

### Aliasing

Harsh, inharmonic fizz that appears when a process like clipping creates frequencies too high for the sample rate, which fold back down into the audible range. Oversampling reduces it.

### Beating

A slow wobble in level heard when two partials are very close in pitch, as they drift in and out of step. Gongs and cracked metal are full of it.

### Ceiling

The highest level Corrosion will output. The diode stage clips a little under it and the limiter stops every sample at it.

### Choke

A host message that stops one note at once. Corrosion fades it out in 2 ms so it doesn't click.

### Coupling

How firmly the exciter and the object push on each other. Set by **Coupling** in Mechanical Linkage.

### Damping

How quickly a mode loses energy. More damping means a shorter ring.

### Drive

Deliberate overloading of a stage to add harmonics and grit. Corrosion has two: Legacy Drive and the Overdrive Unit.

### Envelope

A shape over time. In Corrosion the envelopes shape the exciter's force, not the volume: AD for struck exciters, MSEG for friction, ADSR for specialty.

### Even harmonics

Partials at even multiples of the fundamental (the octave, two octaves up…). Asymmetric clipping adds them, and they sound full and warm.

### Exciter

Whatever plays the object: a hand, a mallet, a bow, a jet of air. See [Exciters](/wiki/exciters/).

### Fundamental

The lowest mode of a note, the one you hear as its pitch.

### General MIDI

A standard layout that puts each drum sound on a fixed key (kick on C2, snare on D2…), so drum patterns play the right sounds on any instrument that follows it.

### Harmonic

Partials at whole-number multiples of the fundamental. Strings and pipes are nearly harmonic, which is why they play clear pitches and chords.

### Inharmonic

Partials that don't line up as whole-number multiples. Bells, plates, cogs and chains are inharmonic: that's their shimmer, and why chords on them can clash.

### Knee

Where a clipper's curve starts to bend. A hard knee clips abruptly; a soft knee bends early and gently.

### Latency

Delay added by processing. Corrosion adds none with the Hard limiter and about 1 ms with Lookahead, which it reports to the host.

### Lorenz attractor

A small chaotic system that wanders endlessly without repeating. The Overdrive Unit uses it to move its drive.

### Modal synthesis

Building a sound from a set of resonating modes, each with its own frequency, level and decay. It's how Corrosion models its objects.

### Mode

One of the ways an object can vibrate, each at its own frequency, with its own level and decay. You hear each mode as a partial.

### MSEG

Multi-stage envelope generator: the six-stage, loopable envelope of the friction exciters. See [Force envelope and MSEG](/wiki/exciters/envelopes/).

### Node

A point on an object where a mode doesn't move. Striking at a mode's node leaves that mode silent.

### Object

The piece of metal that rings: a pipe, a plate, a cable… See [Objects](/wiki/objects/).

### Oversampling

Running a process at a multiple of the sample rate, so the harsh frequencies it creates can be filtered out before they alias.

### Pad

In Kit mode, the sound stored on one key. See [Kit](/wiki/note-modes/kit/).

### Partial

One of the pitches a sound is made of. A note's partials, and how they're spaced, are what make a pipe sound like a pipe.

### Release snap

The jump in level when a held friction note is released quickly and the object springs back. See [Troubleshooting](/wiki/troubleshooting/#a-loud-release).

### Voice

One playing note: an exciter and an object. Corrosion plays up to 16 at once.

### Voice stealing

When more than 16 notes are playing, reusing a voice for the new note: first one that's fading out, then one held by the pedal, then the quietest held note.
