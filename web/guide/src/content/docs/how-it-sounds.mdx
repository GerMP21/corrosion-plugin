Every note in Corrosion is a small physical event: something hits, scrapes or blows on a metal object, the object rings, and the output chain shapes the result.

**Try it:** in the [web app](https://corrosion-vst.org/play/), hold the `A` key, then switch the **EXCITER**'s **MODEL TYPE** to **Bow** and hold it again. Same object, same chain: only what drives the object changed.

Read it left to right. The first three boxes happen once per note (a **voice**), and everything from the voice mix on happens once for all the notes together. The two-way arrow is the heart of the instrument: the exciter doesn't just play a sound into the object, they push each other.

## The voice

When you play a note, Corrosion starts a voice: one exciter and one object.

- **The exciter** is what plays the object. There are <Data count="exciters" />: hands, mallets, a drumstick, a bow, grinders, a jet of air, a mains hum and more. You choose it in **MODEL TYPE**. See [Exciters](/wiki/exciters/).
- **The object** is what rings. There are <Data count="objects" />: a pipe, a plate, a tank, a cable and more. You choose it in **MATERIAL**. See [Objects](/wiki/objects/).

An object rings at many pitches at once, its *partials*. Their spacing is what makes a pipe sound like a pipe and a plate like a plate. Each partial has its own decay, so the sound changes colour as it fades: high partials usually die first.

The exciter and the object are coupled. Every sample, the exciter feels how far and how fast the object is moving and pushes back. That's why a bow catches and slips on the metal instead of just playing a recorded scrape, and why a mallet sounds different on a plate than on a cable.

A few things change what the voice does from note to note:

- **Velocity.** Harder notes push harder and sound brighter. On the friction exciters (Bow and the scrapers), the **Gesture Modifiers** under the exciter also decide how much velocity changes the envelope's level and timing.
- **Releasing the key** lets go of the exciter, not the object. A struck pipe keeps ringing after you let go until its own damping stops it; a bowed one rings on after the bow leaves.
- **The pitch** comes from the key and the object's size: the larger the object, the lower it sounds.

Up to 16 notes play at once. When you play more, Corrosion takes over the quietest voice that is already fading out, then one the sustain pedal holds, then the quietest held note.

## The output chain

All the voices are mixed into one stereo signal, which then goes through the chain, in this order. The controls are in the **PROCESSING** column and the header; [Output chain](/wiki/output-chain/) covers each stage.

1. **Legacy Drive** (in the Overdrive Unit) saturates the voice mix before anything else.
2. **Filter Bank:** a resonant low-pass filter. Lower **Cutoff** darkens everything.
3. **Overdrive Unit:** a drive whose amount is wobbled by a chaotic system, from warm saturation to sputtering distortion.
4. **BODY:** a resonating body added under the sound, as if the object stood in a machine. **Chassis Mat** and **Chassis Vol** in the Dispersion panel set its tone; the header's **BODY** fader sets how much you hear.
5. **Spread:** **Spread** and **Proximity** place the sound in the stereo field, wider and nearer or narrower and further.
6. **Space:** a factory room, a spring tank or an echo, mixed in with **Amount**.
7. **Diode stage:** a clipper that rounds off the loudest peaks. **Softness** and **Asymmetry** in the Brickwall Limiter panel set its character.
8. **OUTPUT:** the final level, in dB.
9. **Limiter:** keeps every peak under the **Ceiling**. The header's **LIMITER** chooses a hard clip or a lookahead limiter.

The four **Macros** above the columns reach into both halves: they move controls of the exciter, the object and the chain at once. See [Macros](/wiki/macros/).
