A coiled spring: inharmonic and chirpy, with a wobble that makes basses and leads bounce. Flick a door-stop spring and you'll hear it: the high and low frequencies travel along it at different speeds and arrive at different times, a "pew" that's pure physics.

**Try it:** load **Bass → Coil Spring Bass**, then raise **Spring Slosh** between notes.

## What it is

A helical spring. High frequencies travel along it at a different speed from low ones, so a hit smears into a chirp, and the coil wobbles as it rings. Its modes don't form a harmonic series.

## How it sounds

Boingy, wobbly and metallic, with the chirp of a spring reverb. It suits basses and leads with character; being inharmonic, it isn't used for chords.

## Controls

<ModelControls object="Coil Spring" />

- **Coil Length:** the size. Longer coils sound lower. At 1.09 it plays the key; at 1 it's 148 cents sharp.
- **Dispersion Chirp:** how much the spring smears high frequencies. Higher gives a stronger springy chirp.
- **Spring Slosh:** the wobble of the spring. Higher sounds looser and more liquid.

## Pairings

Felt Mallet gives a bass; Bow, a wobbly lead and a drone.

## Tips

- Set **Coil Length** to 1.09 to play in tune.
- For a spring-reverb flavour on a different object, use the **Spring** mode of Space instead ([Space and Echo](/wiki/output-chain/space/)).
- Springs and slow attacks don't mix well: the chirp lives in the transient. Use a struck exciter or Tension Snap to show it off.

## Presets to try

- **Bass → Coil Spring Bass**
- **Lead → Bowed Spring Lead**
- **Drones → Spring Bow Drone**
- **Experimental → *exciter* → *exciter* · Coil Spring**: each of the 16 exciters has one preset on this object.
