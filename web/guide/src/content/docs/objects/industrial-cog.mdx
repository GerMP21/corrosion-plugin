A toothed cog and its blade: dissonant, split modes for gongs, ticks and hums. Its modes come in close, slightly detuned pairs that beat against each other, which is what gives gongs and bells their shimmer and slow wobble.

**Try it:** load **Percussion → Cog Gong Hit**, then raise **Tooth Dissonance** between notes.

## What it is

A large gear wheel. Its disc rings like a blade, and its teeth split the modes into close, slightly detuned pairs, which beat against each other.

## How it sounds

Dissonant, gong-like and mechanical. Struck soft, it gives gongs; struck hard and small, ticks; hummed, a machine drone. It's inharmonic: tune it by ear.

## Controls

<ModelControls object="Industrial Cog" />

- **Blade Radius:** the size. Larger cogs sound lower.
- **Tooth Dissonance:** the detuning of the cog's teeth. Higher sounds more dissonant.
- **Blade Thickness:** the thickness of the cog. Higher sounds brighter and stiffer. It acts on held notes.

## Pairings

Felt Mallet gives a gong; Drumstick, a tick; Electromagnetic Hum, a hum drone.

## Tips

- Lower **Tooth Dissonance** for a cleaner, bell-like tone.
- A small cog with Drumstick makes a clock tick.
- The beating is slow on low notes and fast on high ones. For a gong that breathes, play it low.

## Presets to try

- **Percussion → Cog Gong Hit**
- **Percussion → Cog Tick**
- **Drones → Cog Hum Drone**
- **Experimental → *exciter* → *exciter* · Industrial Cog**: each of the 16 exciters has one preset on this object.
