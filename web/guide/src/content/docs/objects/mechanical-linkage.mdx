Mechanical Linkage sets where and how the exciter touches the object: the point it strikes, how firmly the two are coupled, and how that point moves while a note plays.

All six controls act on held notes.

**Try it:** pick **Bow**, hold a note and raise **Pos Wander**: the timbre drifts as the bow moves along the object.

## Strike Pos

Where the exciter touches the object, from one end (0) to the other (1). Each mode has places where it moves a lot and places where it stays still (its nodes); striking near a node leaves that mode quiet. So the strike point decides which modes ring:

- In the middle, every second mode sits on a node, which gives a hollower, more open tone.
- Near an end, more of the upper modes ring, for a brighter, thinner tone.
- The two halves mirror each other: 0.2 sounds like 0.8.

At the very ends the fundamental would vanish; **Anchor** keeps it.

<Clips param="strike_position" />

## Coupling

How strongly the exciter and the object push each other, from <Data param="coupling_stiffness" field="min" /> to <Data param="coupling_stiffness" field="max" />. At the maximum (the default) the force reaches each mode according to the strike point; lower blends toward driving every mode alike, so the strike point matters less. Higher makes their interaction stronger and wilder.

<Clips param="coupling_stiffness" />

## Pos Wander

How much the strike point drifts while the note plays, as a slow sine around **Strike Pos**. Higher makes the timbre move more. It suits exciters that keep touching the object, like Bow or Heavy Grinding; a strike lands once, so it hears only where the point was at that moment.

<Clips param="position_wander" />

## Wander Rate

The speed of that drift, from <Data param="position_wander_rate" field="range" />. Higher wanders faster. It does nothing while **Pos Wander** is at 0.

<Clips param="position_wander_rate" />

## Pos Env

How much the force envelope sweeps the strike point. As the envelope rises, the point moves away from **Strike Pos**, by up to half the object's length at the maximum, and comes back as the envelope falls. Higher gives a stronger timbral sweep. With a bow swell it moves through the note; with a strike, it's set by the envelope's shape over the hit.

<Clips param="position_envelope" />

## Anchor

How firmly the fundamental keeps its level wherever the strike point is, from <Data param="fundamental_anchor" field="min" /> to <Data param="fundamental_anchor" field="max" />. Near an end of the object, or on a node of the fundamental, the note would lose its pitch; the anchor holds the fundamental at a minimum level. Higher keeps notes more in tune. Away from the ends it changes little.

<Clips param="fundamental_anchor" />
