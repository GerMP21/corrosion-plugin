**Space** puts the sound somewhere. You get three rooms: a big factory hall, a spring reverb tank, and an echo. Only one plays at a time, and each shows its own controls when it's selected.

**Try it:** load **Chords → Cable Echo Pad**, hold a chord and switch **Mode** between **Factory**, **Spring** and **Echo**. Same chord, three different buildings.

## Mode and Amount

**Mode** picks the effect: **Off** (dry), **Factory**, **Spring** or **Echo**. **Amount** is the mix, from <Data param="space_amount" field="min" /> (dry) to <Data param="space_amount" field="max" />; higher sounds wetter. At the default Amount of 0 nothing is added, whatever the mode, so if you pick a mode and hear no difference, check Amount first.

<Clips param="space_amount" />

One quirk worth knowing: every mode adds faint low partials of its own, around 100–145 Hz. Under most sounds they're invisible, but under a quiet, sustained plate they can show up as a low hum. That's why the factory plate chords are dry. If a reverb seems to muddy a delicate patch, that's the reason; a little less Amount usually fixes it.

## Factory

A large factory hall: hard surfaces, lots of space, plenty of junk lying around.

- **Factory Size:** the size of the room. Bigger means a longer, larger reverb.
- **Machinery:** how cluttered the room is. More clutter means busier, denser reflections.
- **Wall Impedance:** how hard the walls are. Higher reflects more of the highs, so it sounds brighter.

**Tip:** for percussion, keep **Factory Size** small and **Amount** low: you get a sense of a place without washing out the attack.

## Spring

A spring reverb, like the tank in an old guitar amp, with its characteristic twang and drip.

- **Spring Tension:** how tight the spring is. Tighter raises the twang.
- **Wire Stiffness:** how stiff the coils are. Stiffer sounds brighter and tighter.
- **Tank Size:** the size of the tank. Larger tanks sound lower.

**Tip:** spring reverbs love transients. Hit it with **Drumstick** or **Hard Mallet** and a high **Amount** for that boingy, surf-guitar splash.

## Echo

A stereo echo, free-running or locked to your host's tempo, with a bit of tape-style wobble if you want it.

- **Sync:** locks the echo to the host tempo, from 1/16 to 2/1 of a beat. **Off** uses **Delay Time** instead, and so does a host that doesn't report a tempo.
- **Delay Time:** the time between echoes, from about 20 ms to 800 ms. Very short times thicken the sound like a doubler; long times give distinct repeats.
- **Movement:** how much the delay time wobbles. Higher sounds more warped, like a worn tape machine.
- **HF Damping:** darkens each repeat, so later echoes get duller, just as they would in an old analogue delay. The effect lives in the repeats: under the dry note it's subtle, so listen to the tail.
