Physical modelling is hungry work: every note is dozens of resonating modes, each computed every sample, for up to 16 voices. **MODE**, in the header, trades detail for CPU.

**Try it:** play a dense chord on **Chords → Bowed Cable Pad** in **Normal**, then switch **MODE** to **Render** and play it again. Listen for the extra shimmer in the upper partials, and watch your host's CPU meter.

## Modes

Two things change with the mode: how many modes each voice gets, and how much the diode stage oversamples to keep its clipping clean.

| Mode | Modes per voice | Diode oversampling | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eco** | half | none | Big projects, slow computers, sketching |
| **Normal** (default) | the standard set | 4× | Everyday playing and mixing |
| **High** | 1.5× | 8× | Detailed sounds, exposed solos |
| **Render** | 2× | 16× | Bouncing and final mixdown |

More modes mean richer, more detailed upper partials, most audible on bright objects like **Plate** and **Sheet Metal**. More oversampling means less aliasing (the harsh, inharmonic fizz a clipper can fold back into the audio) when the diode stage is working hard.

The two parts switch at different times. The oversampling changes immediately. The mode count is set when a note starts, so notes already sounding keep theirs and the next notes get the new one.

Presets store a mode, and the factory presets all use **Normal**: loading one sets **MODE** back to Normal.

## Latency

- The **Hard** limiter adds no latency.
- The **Lookahead** limiter adds about 1 ms, which Corrosion reports to your host so the track stays in time.
- The diode stage's oversampling delays the sound by a few samples (a tenth of a millisecond or less), which is too small to matter and isn't reported.

## Saving CPU

Corrosion only spends CPU on voices that are still sounding, so silence is cheap and dense, long-ringing chords are expensive. If your computer struggles:

1. **Switch to Eco while you write,** and back to Render for the final bounce. Remember that loading a preset sets Normal again.
2. **Play fewer overlapping notes,** or shorten tails: long-ringing objects (Pipe, I-Beam, Taut Cable) keep voices busy for seconds, and **Rust** or **Sludge** frees them sooner.
3. **Freeze or bounce** finished parts in your host.
4. **In the web app,** everything runs on one core of the browser's audio thread, so the heaviest presets with many notes can be too much for a slower computer. The CPU figure in the web app's bar turns red near the limit.
